# AI-10 · The music dips while you talk to Keys
Design proposal · assistant-dev · 22 Nov 2026 · size **M** (about 8 hours across three developers)
Research used: RES-7 part B and its "For the devs (AI-10)" notes (depth, timing, hold, "never duck during a take", and the Chrome echo-cancelled mic track).

## 1. What and why
When the beat is loud, the microphone hears the music as loudly as your voice, so Keys mishears or misses what you say. With this change, the music drops by about 15 dB (roughly a quarter as loud) the moment you say "Keys" or tap the orb. It stays down while you talk and while Keys answers, then slides back up over just under a second, like a DJ's talkover. On Chrome, Keys also listens through an echo-cancelled microphone that removes the studio's own sound, so it hears "Keys" more easily even before the music dips. When you sing or record a part, the music never dips.

## 2. User flow

**A. Always listening on, a loop playing (the main case)**
1. A 124 bpm loop is playing at full volume. "Always listening for my name" is ticked.
2. You say: "Keys, make the bass softer."
3. As soon as Keys recognises "Keys" (usually 0.3 to 0.8 seconds after you say it), the music drops 15 dB in about 75 ms. You hear a quick smooth dip with no click. A small tag next to the status line reads **Music down 15 dB**, and the level meters drop with it.
4. The music stays down while you finish the sentence. The status line shows "Hearing: make the bass softer…".
5. Keys works on the request (the orb shows thinking). The music stays down.
6. Keys says "Bassline down 4 dB, everything else as it was." While it speaks, the music comes up a little to -10 dB, so you can hear the groove under the voice. The tag reads **Music down 10 dB**.
7. Half a second after Keys stops talking, the music slides back to full over about 0.8 s. The tag disappears. Now you hear the change at full volume.
8. Follow-up: for 8 seconds after a reply, you can talk without saying "Keys" (this already works today). If you start talking in that window ("…and a bit more reverb"), the music dips again as soon as your words are recognised.

**B. Saying only "Keys"**
1. You say "Keys". The music dips, and Keys says "Yes?".
2. The music stays down for the 9-second answer window, so you can speak your request into a quiet room.
3. If you say nothing, the music comes back when the window ends.

**C. Tapping the orb (always listening off)**
1. You tap the orb. The music dips straight away, because the tap is a clear sign you are about to speak.
2. You speak. From here, steps A4 to A7 apply.
3. If Keys hears nothing ("I didn't hear anything"), the music comes back after the 0.5 s hold.

**D. Typing**
- Typing in the box never dips the music: you type while listening.
- If "Speak replies" is on, the music dips to -10 dB only while Keys reads its answer out, then comes back.

**E. Singing or playing a part (never dips)**
1. You say: "Keys, let me sing the bassline." The music dips while you say it and while Keys answers "Sing after the count-in."
2. The moment the count-in starts, the music snaps back to full in about 0.2 s, faster than the normal release, so the count-in clicks are at full level.
3. During the count-in, the take and any keyboard recording or overdub, nothing dips. Only the drums play, as today.
4. Humming over a playing loop (the "hum becomes a track" feature) never dips the music either: humming isn't talking, and you need the loop to hum in time.

**F. Talk that isn't for Keys**
- Talking to someone in the room without saying "Keys" does not dip the music (with the recommended trigger, decision D2).

**G. Settings**
- A new menu in the assistant options reads **Music dips when I talk: Off / Light / Normal / Deep**. Normal is the default. Your choice is remembered in this browser.
  - Light: -8 dB while you talk, -6 dB while Keys speaks.
  - Normal: -15 dB while you talk, -10 dB while Keys speaks.
  - Deep: -24 dB while you talk, -15 dB while Keys speaks.
  - Off: no dip. The echo-cancelled microphone still helps on Chrome.
- Safety net: if anything goes wrong (an error, a lost reply), the music is never left down for more than 20 seconds.
- Downloads (MP3), sung takes and recordings are never affected: the dip happens only on the speakers.

## 4. How it will be built

**sound-engine-dev: `src/core.js` (about 1 h)**
- Add one gain node, `A.duck`, at the very end of the chain: `A.comp → A.duck → A.analyser → speakers`. It sits after the glue compressor and the limiter, because before them the glue compressor would undo about half of the dip.
  - Since everything (tracks, metronome clicks, Sound Lab previews) passes through it, everything dips together.
  - The meters and scope sit after it, so they show the dip.
- Add `duckMusic(db, tau=0.025)` and `unduckMusic(tau=0.25)`. Both use the existing `holdParam`, then `setTargetAtTime`; they never set `.value` directly. Add `A.duckDb`, which holds the current target in dB.
- Use a new name: this is not the kick sidechain `duckAt` in `src/mixer.js`.
- The offline render (`renderAudio`, MP3 export) doesn't use the duck, so exports are untouched.
- Only if the owner picks "keep the kick" (D3 option B): add one low-shelf filter, `A.duckShelf`, at 160 Hz. Its boost goes from 0 to +9 dB at the same moment as the dip, so the kick and sub only drop about 6 dB.

**assistant-dev: `src/assistant.js` (about 4-5 h)**
1. **Duck controller.** One small object (`TALKDUCK`) with `down(kind)` and `up(holdMs)`.
   - `kind` is `talk` or `reply`. The depth comes from the setting (stored as `asDuck`).
   - It does nothing (or releases at once with tau 0.08) while `LOOP.state` is `take` or `counting`, or while `LOOP.capturing()` is true.
   - It keeps a 20 s safety timer.
   - It shows and hides the tag, but only while the loop or song is playing (`TR.running`).
2. **Triggers:**
   - **Always listening:** in `rec.onresult`, dip on the first interim result where `findWake(interim)!==null`. Also dip when `isSpeechy(interim)` is true inside the follow-up window (`performance.now()<ASSIST.awakeUntil`).
   - **Orb tap:** dip in the click handler, before the recogniser starts.
   - **Talk that isn't for Keys:** release on `onFinal` when the words weren't addressed to Keys (the "Start with Keys" branch).
   - **Tap ending empty:** release on `tapRec` `onerror` or `onend` when no request is running.
   - **While working:** `handleRequest` keeps the dip while busy if it was voice-started. A typed request doesn't dip.
   - **While Keys speaks:** `speak()` sets the `reply` depth. Its `done()` calls `up(500)`.
   - **Silent replies:** with "Speak replies" off, release 500 ms after the reply bubble.
   - **Bare "Keys":** hold the dip until the 9 s window ends, then release.
   - **Sung take:** release fast in `TAKE.start` (via `afterSpeak`) and on `stopListening()`.
3. **Echo-cancelled mic for recognition (Chrome).**
   - When listening starts (or on an orb tap), open one assistant mic stream with `{echoCancellation:true, noiseSuppression:true, autoGainControl:true}`.
   - Call `rec.start(track)` on the first start and on every Chrome auto-restart in `onend`.
   - Open it only in Chromium 135 or newer (checked with `navigator.userAgentData.brands`). `start.length` can't be used as a test, because it is 0 even where a track is accepted (checked). Safari and others keep `rec.start()` and get the dip only.
   - Close the track in `stopListening()`, `killRec`/`dropRec` and at the end of an orb tap, so the browser's mic indicator goes off exactly as AI-7 requires.
   - The singing and humming mic in `src/mic.js` stays separate, with echo cancellation off (right for pitch).
   - If `getUserMedia` or `start(track)` fails, fall back to `rec.start()` silently.
4. No new assistant actions, no Worker or prompt change, and nothing in `actions.js` or `maker.js`.
   - Possible follow-up, not in this item: a voice command ("Keys, don't turn the music down") would need an action in all three files plus a smoke line.

**studio-ux-dev: `src/body.html`, `src/head.html` (about 1 h)**
- Add the "Music dips when I talk" `<select id="asDuck">` in `.as-opts` and the `<span class="as-duck" hidden>` tag after `#asStatus`, styled as in the mockup.
- When UX-4 (phone tabs) lands, the menu goes with the other Keys options in the phone Keys tab.
- Check the layout at 390 and 360 px.
- Optional: assistant-dev can make these two small markup additions itself if the lead allows it in the build brief.

**Testing (assistant-dev during build, then qa-tester)**
- **Headless, with a fake recogniser:**
  - An interim "keys make the…" moves `A.duck.gain` to about 0.18 within 100 ms.
  - The gain is held while busy and goes to about 0.32 during the reply.
  - It is back to 1.0 within 1.5 s after the reply ends.
  - It stays at 1.0 through a count-in and a sung take.
  - It stays at 1.0 for typed requests with voice off.
  - The 20 s cap works.
  - `stopListening()` leaves every mic track `ended`.
  - Smoke-test lines: "talking to Keys dips the music and brings it back" and "a sung take never dips".
- **Headless, two mics at once (already checked in Chrome 153 with the fake mic):**
  - An echo-cancelled track and the singing track (echo cancellation off) can be open together, and each keeps its own settings.
  - `rec.start(track)` is accepted.
- **Real use (owner or tester, laptop speakers, loud 124 bpm loop):**
  - Wake-word hits out of 20 tries in three setups: no dip; dip; dip plus the echo-cancelled mic.
  - Then the same on the owner's iPhone.

## 5. Effort, risks, decisions

**Effort: M, about 8 hours.**
- assistant-dev: 4-5 h.
- sound-engine-dev: 1 h.
- studio-ux-dev: 1 h.
- qa-tester: 1-2 h.

Order: sound-engine's node first (small), then assistant-dev, with studio-ux-dev's markup in parallel. AI-13 ("Saying Keys wakes it every time") edits the same listening code: whichever ships second rebases onto the first.

**Measured (offline render of the studio's own drums, bass, chords and melody at 124 bpm; peak -0.6 dBFS):**

The dips at each level are exact: Normal is -15.0 dB in every band, Light -8.0 dB and Deep -24.0 dB. The timing:
- The dip reaches 95% in 75 ms.
- The return reaches 95% in 749 ms.
- The largest gain step is 0.0007 per sample, so it doesn't click.

How the three styles in D3 lower each band (dB):

| Style | 63 Hz | 125 Hz | 250 Hz | 500 Hz | 1 kHz | 2 kHz | 4 kHz | 8 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain dip, Normal | -15 | -15 | -15 | -15 | -15 | -15 | -15 | -15 |
| Keep the kick (-15 with a 160 Hz shelf) | -6 | -7 | -10 | -14 | -15 | -15 | -15 | -15 |
| DJ mid cut (-6 overall, -10 dB at 1.5 kHz) | -6 | -6 | -6.5 | -8 | -12 | -14 | -9 | -7 |

So the DJ-mixer mid cut that RES-7 suggested helps the voice band much less than expected: only -8 dB at 500 Hz, where our mixes hold their mid energy.

**Risks**
- **iPhone and Safari.** Safari can't give recognition an echo-cancelled track, so it gets the dip only.
  - iOS may already lower the page's sound when the mic opens. Stacked with our dip, the music could get very quiet on a phone.
  - We test on the owner's iPhone at gate 2. If it's too much, phones default to Light.
- **Echo.** Chrome's echo canceller removes only this page's own sound.
  - It can't remove other apps, a second speaker system, or Keys' spoken voice if the browser plays it outside the page. Today's rule (ignore what's heard while Keys speaks) stays.
  - On some Windows PCs, opening a call-style mic triggers Windows' "lower other sounds while communicating" setting. That would make all music quieter for as long as listening is on.
  - If testing shows this, we open the echo-cancelled track only after "Keys" is heard. The first "Keys" over loud music would then not get the help, but the command would.
  - Bluetooth headsets already drop to call quality when any mic opens; this doesn't change that.
- **Pumping.** Music bouncing up and down between phrases would feel cheap. Three things prevent it:
  - The 0.5 s hold.
  - The 0.8 s return.
  - Staying down from your words straight through Keys' reply (it moves only from -15 to -10, never back to full in between).
- **Recognition delay.** "Keys" is recognised 0.3-0.8 s after you say it, so the first word or two of a quick command ("Keys make the bass…") are still heard over full music.
  - The echo-cancelled mic covers this gap on Chrome.
  - Option B in D2 adds a light early dip if needed.
- **Stuck low.** If a reply is lost or an error happens, the 20 s safety cap and the release in `stopListening` bring the music back.
- **Privacy.** No new network calls, and nothing is recorded or sent. The extra mic track is used only by the browser's own speech recognition, which already listens today. It closes whenever listening stops.
- **Can't fully test headless.** Real speech recognition and real echo can't run in the workspace, so the hit-rate test needs a real laptop at gate 2.

**Decisions for the owner**
1. **D1: How much should the music dip by default?**
   - Options: Light (-8 dB) · **Normal (-15 dB, recommended)** · Deep (-24 dB).
   - Normal sits between phones (-14) and the Pioneer DJ mixer default (-18). It moves "music as loud as your voice" to a level where recognition mostly works, and the groove is still clearly there. The user can change it in the menu.
2. **D2: What makes it dip?**
   - **A. Only "Keys", an orb tap, or follow-up words right after a reply (recommended).**
   - B. A, plus a light -6 dB early dip whenever real words are heard, even without "Keys". It lifts after 2.5 s if no "Keys" follows. Humming doesn't count.
   - C. Any sound on the mic, including humming and singing along.
   - A never dips for chat in the room or for humming along. B catches the first words better but also dips when you talk to a friend. C would dip every time you hum along, which fights the hum-to-track feature.
3. **D3: What kind of dip?**
   - **A. A plain volume dip (recommended).** It cuts the voice band by the full 15 dB, which is what helps Keys hear.
   - B. "Keep the kick": the kick and sub drop only 6 dB, and everything from 250 Hz up drops 14-15 dB. This feels more like a club talkover, but low thump still reaches the mic.
   - C. The DJ-mixer mid cut. It measured weakest on our mixes (only -8 dB at 500 Hz), so it isn't recommended.
   - B can be added later as a menu choice ("Normal, keep the kick") if the owner misses the groove.
